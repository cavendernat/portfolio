WHO Mortality Database Usability Study

A team usability evaluation designed to test whether users could efficiently navigate, filter, interpret, and compare mortality data in the World Health Organization's database interface.

RoleTechnical setup, scribe, observer; team research projectMethods / toolsThink-aloud · task analysis · interviews · surveys · efficiency/error metrics · heuristic evaluationPortfolio noteThis case study only claims details supported by the available project materials.

Research goals

The study examined usability, navigability, ease of use, barriers to interpreting data, fit with user needs, and feedback/error-prevention mechanisms. Research questions focused on what worked, what failed, task completion, and whether users understood terminology.

Evaluation design

The plan combined task-based testing with think-aloud observation, interviews, surveys, screen/audio recording, and post-session analysis. Measures included time to completion, number of steps/clicks, task success, assistance required, error rate and severity, and perceived ease of use.

Representative task

One task required users to locate mortality data for renal/kidney disease in Japan. The analysis anticipated a terminology mismatch: an ordinary user might search for “renal disease” without knowing to navigate through “Genitourinary diseases” to nephritis/nephrosis.

My role

My documented team responsibilities were scribing, technical setup, and observation. During the session workflow, the scribe captured notes from interviews, observations, and the think-aloud process.

Why it belongs here: this project shows the evaluation side of my work—turning broad questions about usability into observable tasks, defined metrics, structured data collection, and actionable interface findings.
